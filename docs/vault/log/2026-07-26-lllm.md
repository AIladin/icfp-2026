## 00:30 — three changes to the 25th's plan, before laying the CPU

**Hypothesis**: the CPU can be laid out as written *if* the interpreted direction is renumbered
0..3, because that turns the `X` opcode from a pair of 4-leaf lookup chains into seven cells of
arithmetic, and the pipe-binding risk dissolves once you accept that ticks are free.

1. **Direction is 0..3 (E,S,W,N), 4 = halted**, not 1..4/5. `X` then becomes
   `rM1+M3&` for clockwise and `rM3+M3&` for counter-clockwise — `(dir±1) & 3` — instead of two
   more equality chains. Ops stay 1-based (op 1..4 push `op-1` as the direction) because the `@`
   marker is a *negated* op and op 0 could not be negated.

2. **The three display pipes get equal length.** The 25th's note wanted
   `L_addr = L_data + cells-walked` so the ADDR/DATA pair lands on one tick. That is unnecessary and
   it is also *wrong* for the main loop, which sends ADDR,DATA,ADDR,DATA,SWAP: a short ADDR pipe
   lets the second ADDR overtake the first DATA and the restore paints at the new address. With all
   three lengths equal, arrivals are in program order by construction, which is all the display
   needs (it processes ADDR→DATA→SWAP within a tick, so same-tick is not required).

3. **Ticks are free, so walk between sends.** The cap is 15M and the machine needs ~300k. Nearest-pipe
   between the CPU's three outgoing pipes is therefore not a geometry puzzle: put each pipe on its own
   wall far from the others and let a leaf walk 30 cells before its second send. This replaces the
   25th's "two pipes into a demultiplexing bus room" — no extra room, no ordering constraint between
   `rot` and the opcode.

## 02:00 — the machine is complete; the fight is the *pipe graph*, not the logic

`py/lllm_gen3.py` builds all ten rooms — IN, COLCTL, ROWCTL, TAIL, ROT, SPLIT, ECHO, **CPU**, **EMIT**
and the LM-75. The two missing rooms are written and the whole grid loads. What has eaten the time
is not logic, it is [[Nearest pipe resolution|nearest-pipe]] and the ephemeral router.

### `--audit` is the tool that should have existed first

`uv run python lllm_gen3.py --audit` prints, for every room with more than one pipe per direction,
each `r`/`s` cell, the pipe it binds to and the **margin** over the runner-up. It found that
`lllm_gen2.py`'s **COLCTL was wired wrong in five places** — the second preamble `r` (which reads `H`
from the input) bound the *row-kind* pipe, and the MIDDLE lane's `r` and `s` were both mis-pointed.
That room has been rewritten around its ports rather than the other way round; margins are now 26+.

> [!important] Put two ports on the **same wall** and the row term of the distance cancels
> COLCTL reads the input everywhere but the row kind exactly once, so `A` sits at north column 10
> and `C` at north column 130: the binding is then decided by **column alone** — every read west of
> column 70 is the input, the one east of it is the row kind. The same trick separates the CPU's
> `n` (state, north col 15) from `k` (rotate count, north col 130), which is what let the "walk east
> between sends" idea work with no bus room at all.

### The CPU, as built

200x46. `@` bottom-left; PRIME asks ROT for cell 0 and sets BP=256; LOOPA/MANFOUND/LOOPB do the
257-cell raster lap and leave the man's index in `B`; AFTER hands EMIT `manpos` and colour 0 and
aligns the ring with `` `255` + M `255` & ``; STEP does `r M 0 - X` and climbs an eleven-node
equality chain with inline leaves, falling through to the `X` opcode which branches three ways on
the sign of the interpreted `A` and computes `(dir±1) & 3`. The tail is a five-node chain on the
direction emitting `rot` at column 130 and `delta` at column 185, then `r(e) s(q)` for the round flag.
Margins from `--audit`: 9 at worst, 230 typical.

### Two gotchas worth keeping

1. **`lm`/`lmr` accept hand-drawn pipes *alongside* `--ephemeral-pipes`.** That is what unblocked the
   display: the three LM-75 pipes are drawn explicitly in `build()` so their **relative lengths** can
   be asserted, and the router handles the other 14. Lengths matter — the main loop sends
   ADDR,DATA,ADDR,DATA,SWAP and a short ADDR pipe lets the *second* ADDR overtake the *first* DATA,
   which paints the restore at the new address. The generator now raises unless
   `-181 < L_addr - L_data <= 160` and `L_swap > L_data - 290`, bounds read straight off the cell
   counts between the sends.
2. **A routed pipe that *bends* alongside a room wall re-parses as a second pipe.** With ECHO
   directly above the CPU, the `k` pipe (CPU -> ROT) ran west along ECHO's north wall and its `^`
   bend had ECHO's wall as its backward cell — so the loader read it as a pipe *starting* at ECHO,
   giving ECHO two outgoing pipes and four AMBIGUOUS sends. The fix is placement: keep the corridor
   a long pipe wants out of any room's wall, or move the port to a wall that corridor does not touch.

### Where the display sits

Three pipes out of one wall into three sides of the display is the hardest thing the router faces.
It is only topologically clean when **SWAP's source column lies between ADDR's and DATA's** and the
display sits below: ADDR descends into the top, DATA runs west under everything into the left wall,
SWAP rises into the bottom, and none of the three crosses. `E_ADDR_COL, E_SWAP_COL, E_DATA_COL =
20, 100, 180` encodes exactly that, and the EMIT boot loop was re-ordered (SWAP moved from row 7 to
row 8) so the man still meets the columns in increasing order.

## 04:30 — status: logic done and audited, **packing is the blocker**

All ten rooms build and `--audit` is clean (every `r`/`s` binds where it should, worst margin 9).
The machine has never run end to end because **the pipe graph will not close**: 17 pipes between
10 rooms, and every arrangement tried leaves one or two pipes with no legal route.

`py/lllm_gen3.py` is the marker/router version; `py/lllm_gen4.py` is the hand-routed version, which
draws every pipe explicitly with `pipe(g, [waypoints])` and lets `Grid.put` raise on any collision.
Neither is green. **`lllm_gen4.py` is the one to continue** — hand routing is deterministic and each
failure names the exact colliding cell.

### What is proven and must not be re-derived

- **COLCTL's port geometry** (`A` north col 10, `C` north col 130, `b` north col 15, `d` south col 22,
  `e` south col 130) — margins 26+. `lllm_gen2.py`'s COLCTL was mis-bound in five places.
- **CPU port geometry**: `O` north col 2, `n` north col 10, `k`/`q` south, `E` far from `O` on the
  opposite side. `n` vs `k` on the same wall means the choice is by column alone.
- **EMIT**: `Q` north col 5, `M` east row 25, sends at columns 20 (ADDR), 100 (SWAP), 180 (DATA).
  ADDR/SWAP/DATA in *that* column order is required — see below.
- **Display pipe lengths**: `-181 < L_addr - L_data <= 160`, `L_swap > L_data - 290`,
  `L_swap - L_addr < 300`. The generator asserts it.
- The **ring** needs `len(h) + len(i) >= 256` with slack; 480-650 cells is comfortable and the ring
  latency (length / 256 ticks per value) stays under ROT's 8 ticks per rotation.

### The packing rules learned the hard way

1. **A pipe bend adjacent to a room wall re-parses as a new pipe.** Its backward cell is the wall, so
   the loader reads it as a pipe *starting* at that room. Keep long corridors off room walls.
2. **Two rooms joined by pipes in both directions must be adjacent**, or the two pipes interleave and
   one cannot route. ECHO/CPU and TAIL/ROT are the pairs that matter.
3. **The display's three pipes only route when SWAP's source column lies between ADDR's and DATA's**
   and the display is below EMIT: ADDR descends into the top, DATA runs west into the left wall, SWAP
   rises into the bottom, and nothing crosses.
4. **The room graph is planar** — it is a cycle IN→COLCTL→TAIL→ROT→SPLIT→ECHO→CPU→EMIT→display plus
   two chords that share the CPU. A circular/serpentine floorplan in that order therefore *can* be
   routed with zero crossings; the failures so far are all from rooms sitting in the wrong order.
   **ECHO must sit west of SPLIT** so `o` (ECHO→CPU) is a straight drop that never crosses
   `m` (SPLIT→EMIT).

### Next step for whoever picks this up

Work in `lllm_gen4.py`. Keep the serpentine order and place the rooms so that consecutive rooms in
the cycle are *vertically adjacent bands*, then draw each pipe as a single L or Z inside the gap
between its two bands. The two chords (`e` COLCTL→CPU and `k` CPU→ROT) get dedicated corridors — `k`
down the far west, `e` down the far east — and must not share a row or column with anything else.
The remaining conflict when I stopped was `e` (COLCTL→CPU) against `m` (SPLIT→EMIT); the fix is to
make `m` turn east *below* `e`'s horizontal (m row 300, e row 281 worked on paper).

## 12:30 — the pipe graph closes: reorder the stack, then let the router route

**Hypothesis**: the graph is planar in a *different* path order than the one the 25th assumed, and
in the right order two corridors suffice — so the ephemeral router can do the routing instead of

Counting chords on the path used so far (COLCTL, TAIL, ROT, SPLIT, ECHO, CPU, EMIT) gives three
chords `e`[1,6], `k`[3,6], `m`[4,7] that interleave **pairwise**, so no two-corridor floorplan can
hold them. `lllm_gen3.py`'s *ports* already imply a different order:

```
IN, ROWCTL, COLCTL, TAIL, ROT, CPU, ECHO, SPLIT, EMIT, display
```

because `CPU.k` is on the **north** wall (ROT above), `CPU.O`/`n` on the **south** (ECHO below),
`ECHO.L` on the south (SPLIT below) and `SPLIT.m` on the south (EMIT below). In that order the only
chords are `e` COLCTL→CPU [2,5], `j` ROT→SPLIT [4,7] and `q` CPU→EMIT [5,8]. `e` and `q` **share
CPU**, so they nest; `j` interleaves with both and takes the *west* corridor while they take the
*east*. **Two corridors, zero crossings.**

Two fixes were needed on top of the reorder:

1. **`CPU.n` moved from south col 15 to south col 40.** The `X` leaf's `s` at (68,18) was exactly
   82 cells from both `n` and `k` — `--audit` had flagged the tie and the loader confirmed it
   (`AMBIGUOUS 's' ... reading order picks 'k'`). North distance is `y+2`, south is `47-y`; at col
   40 the margin is 25.
2. **`COLCTL.A` moved from north col 10 to north col 2**, with IN parked four rows above it. `b`
   leaves COLCTL at north col 5 heading *east* to ROWCTL, so it crossed column 11 and walled `a`
   out of its own four-cell drop. Putting `A` west of `b` removes the crossing entirely.

`lllm_gen5.py` now routes **17/17 pipes with no AMBIGUOUS send and no spurious pipe**, and the
`--ephemeral-out` grid loads with no errors. Ring `h`+`i` = 464 cells (needs ≥256); display ADDR
361 / DATA 315 / SWAP 154, inside the ordering bounds.

## 13:40 — GREEN. 21/21 on the server, score 240,358,899,810

Submission `9561ff15-667d-43dc-8222-ef7ed94dcff5`, `programs/lllm-v1-routed.man`,
595,984 (419x772) x 403,297.6 ticks. Local `lmr test` 10/10 in 275k–781k ticks against a 5M cap.

Once the pipes closed, four bugs stood between the machine and green. All four were *logic*, and
none was a deadlock in the sense the fleet expected — the step cap was hiding ordinary wrong
answers plus one genuine desync.

1. **LOOPB read four words per cell, not five.** LOOPA is entered through `rX`, which has already
   taken the op; LOOPB starts cold. `rrsrsrs` -> `rrrsrsrs`. Symptom: the whole request loop
   CPU->ROT->SPLIT->ECHO->CPU starved with every man blocked on a receive.
2. **The SWAP pipe was shorter than the DATA pipe.** EMIT's boot tail sends ADDR, walks 185 cells,
   sends DATA, walks **101**, sends SWAP — so `L_swap` must exceed `L_data - 101`, not the
   `L_data - 290` the generator was asserting. With SWAP at 154 and DATA at 315, frame 0 committed
   before the man's own pixel landed: every case matched except one pixel of frame 0.
3. **EMIT's base colour was one cell behind.** The CPU handed EMIT an explicit initial colour of 0
   *and* SPLIT sent the colour of the alignment pop, so every later restore used the previous
   cell's colour. Fixed by deleting the CPU's `0 s(q)` and letting EMIT take its initial colour
   from SPLIT's first word — which is the `@` cell's colour, i.e. 0, by construction.
4. **Op 11's leaf walked through the `X` leaf's arm and re-executed it.** `JX, XJX = 76, 82` put
   the op chain's join column *east* of the `X` branch at 60, and `Walk.to` does not skip written
   cells — the man executes them. So every interpreted `-` ran an extra `r` and an extra `s` and
   desynchronised the state ring. `JX, XJX, XBR = 58, 78, 62` puts the join west of the branch.

> [!important] `Walk.to` now **raises** when it crosses a written cell
> A walk that passes over a cell does not skip it; the man executes it. `lllm_lay.py:to` now raises
> unless the cell is blank or an arrowhead pointing the way the walker is already going (that case
> is a genuine no-op and is how COLCTL's PAD and EMPTY lanes rejoin). It caught bug 4 instantly and
> would have caught it three hours earlier.

### `room_variants.py` is the wrong generator for this problem, twice over

1. **`lmp` only ever tries `COMBINATIONS = 16` variant combinations at the seed**
   (`rs/crates/packer/src/seed.rs:50`), and its Latin sweep only guarantees coverage within
   `max(variants per instance)` samples. Running `room_variants.py --limit 400` on the four wide
   rooms made seeding *worse*: 16 samples out of a product space of 10^20 never included the
   arrangement the pipe graph wanted. Ten curated variants per type beats four hundred.
2. **It moves one pin at a time** (plus whole-set slides), so it cannot express `rot` with `H`
   west **and** `j` east — which is precisely the pin set a north-to-south stack needs. It also
   silently writes variants where two pins land on the same cell, which `lmp` then rejects one
   file at a time.

`py/lllm_rooms.py` replaces it for these rooms: it sweeps the cross product of per-pin wall/offset
moves and keeps a candidate only if **every `s`/`r`/`q` still reaches the same port it does in the
base room**. That audit is what makes re-pinning safe, and it is brutal — the CPU has exactly one
legal alternative and EMIT two, because their sends are spread over 200 columns and no vertical
wall can serve them. The walls it *does* free up are the ones that mattered:
`rot Hw6-Ke1-is1-je5`, `split Je*`, and `cpu E` on the **north** wall, which removes the corridor
that used to have to wrap all the way around the CPU.

### The seed routed and failed 0/10 — and it was a pipe length, not logic

First routed seed: ADDR 11 cells, DATA 211, SWAP 122. `la - ld = -200` violates the
`-181 < la - ld` bound the 13:40 entry recorded, so the **second ADDR overtook the first DATA** and
every restore painted at the new address. The frame comes out as the man's own trail, shifted one
cell east — a very recognisable picture, worth remembering.

`min = 35` on the ADDR pipe is the entire fix. **Bigger minimums are worse**: `min = 140`/`170`
made the design stop routing altogether, and the contested cells it reported were at COLCTL and
ROT, nowhere near the display — a forced-length pipe eats corridors somewhere else.

### Where the next win is

`lmp` reports the floor as `sqrt(961) ~ 31x31`, so this is not near the area floor at all. It is
near the **room** floor: CPU is 202x48 and EMIT 202x32, so no arrangement can take `max(w,h)` below
202. Packing can reach ~210 (≈13.8G); after that the lever is refolding the CPU's eleven-node
equality chain and EMIT's raster loop into something square, and then ticks.

## 12:00 — `max =` on a pipe is what this design actually wanted

`lmp` gained `max = N` on a `[[pipes]]` entry after this was shipped, and it is the right tool for
the ADDR/DATA/SWAP window that has broken this design twice. The constraint is *relative* —
`-181 < L_addr - L_data <= 160` and `L_swap > L_data - 101` — so no single bound expresses it, but
a `min`/`max` pair on each of the three pins them into a window that satisfies it for every
arrangement instead of for one measured seed:

```toml
[[pipes]]                 # measured: ADDR 35, DATA 225, SWAP 124 on the current seed
from = "emit.data"
to   = "lm75.data"
min  = 80
max  = 200
[[pipes]]
from = "emit.addr"
to   = "lm75.addr"
min  = 60                 # > DATA_max - 181 = 19, and > 0
max  = 220                # <= DATA_min + 160 = 240
[[pipes]]
from = "emit.swap"
to   = "lm75.swap"
min  = 110                # > DATA_max - 101 = 99
```

That would replace the `min = 55` / `min = 140` pair, which are calibrated against one measured
DATA length and silently stop being correct when the library is regenerated. Note the implementer's
caveat: `max` can stop a design seeding, because the seed sweep spreads rooms out to find a route
and spreading is what makes routes long — the fix is a `hint.json` that puts the LM-75 next to
EMIT, which this design already has.
