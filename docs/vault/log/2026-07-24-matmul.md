## Spec TL;DR

MAC counts: 8, 12, 64, 4096, 512, 120, 315 — the 16³ case dominates the average.

Standings `best` 586,350,090 at 00:20, **311,690,333** by 02:40.

## Design (v1)

Loop order `i` (row of A) outer, `t` inner, `j` innermost — the only order where A is consumed
strictly in arrival order and B is replayed. So:

- **A queue**: a straight pipe of `N*M+1` cells, LOADER → MUL. Values raw.
- **B ring**: a delay line holding `B` row-major with a `PAD=1` after every row and one `ENDPAD=3`
  at the very end. B values are stored **doubled** (`2b`) so data is even and markers are odd —
  `b`,`x` classifies in two ticks and clobbers no hand.
- **acc ring**: `K` accumulators plus an odd `MARK=1`, in lockstep with the B ring.

MUL holds `a = A[i][t]` in B, pushes every ring token straight back, and sends `2ab` to ACC. On PAD
it forwards `1` as a control word and pulls the next `a`; on ENDPAD it forwards `3` = dump.
ACC: `t=0` pushes fresh products (INIT), later `t` accumulate, ENDPAD dumps `acc>>1` to output and
empties the ring. Output is `acc>>1` because every product carries the factor 2.

Rooms: IN, LOADER, PADK, TAIL, MUL, ACC, ACCR, OUT.
Gating: MUL must not touch the ring until B is fully loaded, else TAIL's `R` interleaves the loader
stream with recirculated tokens. A one-token **GATE** pipe LOADER→MUL, sent after phase B, does it.

Counters: LOADER computes `N*M` (BP) with `M` surviving in B, then `M*K` parked in B during phase A.
PADK holds `K` in B and counts with BP. That is the whole reason PADK is a separate room — a room
has exactly one usable counter.

## The bug that cost 1/20: TAIL's `R` merges a live ring with a draining loader

TAIL takes the loader stream (from PADK) and MUL's recirculated ring tokens on two incoming pipes
and merges them with `R`. That is only safe if MUL never recirculates while the loader is still
feeding. The GATE token was supposed to guarantee that, but LOADER sends GATE the moment phase B's
last `s` *completes* — the last few B values are still in flight through the LOADER→PADK pipe, PADK's
own loop and the PADK→TAIL pipe, ~86 ticks of drain. The GATE pipe was 32 cells, so MUL woke ~50
ticks early, popped a token off the (already full) ring-back drum and pushed it back to TAIL, which
`R` then interleaved with the tail of the loader stream. One duplicated token = the whole B ring
shifted by one.

Symptom: `M=14..16, K=2` only — a one-slot phase shift in the accumulator ring, and the dump then
terminated one element early. Wide K hid it because the tick arithmetic happened to land the other
way.

**Fix: a 150-cell serpentine delay line spliced into the GATE pipe** (`GATE_DELAY` in the generator),
taking it from 32 to 174 cells. Latency is the synchronisation primitive here — there is no other.
94 fuzzed shapes across the whole `2 ≤ N,M,K ≤ 16` box pass afterwards.

## Layout notes

The room graph is planar — `LOADER–PADK–TAIL–MUL–LOADER` with the A drum parallel to GATE on the
LOADER–MUL side and `rf` parallel to the ring drum on the TAIL–MUL side. Several hours were lost
trying embeddings where the A drum sat *inside* that cycle; every such attempt needs a pipe crossing
and no router can save you. Draw the graph first.

A BFS pipe router lives in the generator. Two rules it enforces:
- the first body cell of a pipe may not turn (it must point away from the source room's wall), and
- no bend may have a room border directly behind it, or the loader reads it as a second pipe start.

## ACC's ACCUM loop: 12 → 10 cells, server 50,159,818

ACC's accumulate lane was a 4x4 ring (perimeter 12) and it, not MUL's 10, set the tick rate: every
one of the `N*M*(K+1)` ring tokens pays it. The lane needs 7 instructions — `r`(prod) `b` `x` `M`
`r`(accb) `+` `s`(accf) — and a rectilinear loop needs 4 corners, but **`x` is itself a turn**, so
only 3 corners are wasted: **10 cells is the floor**, as a 3x4 ring. A 4x3 also has 6 middle slots
but puts the prod-read one row too low, and `r` then binds to the wrong pipe.

Pipe selection in ACC is pure geometry and worth writing down: with `out` at south `x16`, `accf` at
south `x24`, `s` picks `accf` **iff x >= 21** (the `(52-y)` term cancels); `r` picks prod iff
`|x-30|+(y-38) < |x-18|+(52-y)`. The prod read at `(24,44)` wins by 1 — that margin is the reason
the loop cannot simply be shifted down.

Shortening the lane freed row `y51` entirely, so ACC went 22x14 → 22x13 and the whole bottom band
came up a row: **57x59 = 3,481**, local avg ticks 13,230 → 11,700, 16³ case 65,467 → 57,651.

**Server: 20/20, 50,159,818 = 3,481 (57x59) x 14,409.6.** Submission

**Where the floor is.** 58 rows = LOADER 11 + head 2 + drum band 14 + gate drop 2 + MUL 7 + A-wrap 1

even length, MUL needs 6 instructions and ACC 7, and `x` is the only instruction that pays for its
own corner. Ticks are within ~30% of `10 x N*M*(K+1)`, the rest being the PAD/DUMP lanes.

### Where the ~39 ticks per PAD actually went (measured, not fitted)

A per-cell occupancy profiler (`_execute_all` hook, counts each man's cell and whether he was
blocked) on 16x16x16, 57,945 ticks total:

| man | busy | blocked | biggest block |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LOADER | 6,217 | 14 | — |
| MUL | 48,219 | 9,725 | 6,348 at the **gate `r`** — one startup stall |
| ACC | 50,260 | 7,684 | 6,369 at the INIT prod `r` — the same startup stall |

So the run is **~6,350 ticks of load/gate startup, then ~51,600 ticks in which ACC is 97% busy**.
ACC is the bottleneck; MUL is 2,000 behind it. The 10-cell hot loop accounts for 43,680; the
6,580 of overhead was:

- **dump lane 12 cells x N*K = 3,072** — `r b x | M 1 v W } > s` : four of those cells only build
  `B=1` for the `}`.
- **ACCUM PAD lane 20 cells x N(M-1) = 4,800** — the branch walked all the way out to its own
  `r`(accb)/`s`(accf) pair and back.
- **INIT lane 12 cells x N*K = 3,072** — a 12-cell ring doing 4 instructions' work.

### The three fixes

1. **Hoist `B=1` out of the dump loop.** `1`,`M` now sit on the ENDPAD entry walk (16 passes);
   nothing in the dump loop writes B, so `}` just works. Dump ring is **8 cells**: `r b x | } s`
   in a 4x2 at x15..18 y36..37, `x` doubling as the fourth corner.
2. **Merge the PAD branch back into the ACCUM ring.** PAD no longer has its own ring `r`/`s`: it
   sets `0`,`M` (A=0, B=0) and rejoins at the ring's `>` so the ring's own `r`(accb) `+` `s`(accf)
   does the rotation with a no-op add. **20 -> 16 cells.**
3. **INIT is a 4-instruction ring** (`r b x | s`) at x27..30 y33..34 — **12 -> 8 cells.**

MUL's marker return legs were also straightened: the PAD leg was a 26-cell lap around the whole
room (`s` prod, `r` A, `M`, then 16 cells of travel); it now runs east along the bottom row and
climbs the ring's own `^` column — **26 -> 16**. ENDPAD likewise 26 -> 16.

16x16x16: 57,945 -> 53,839. Every case improved: 891/957/2001/10935/3257/6321 ->
851/907/1807/9039/2865/5587.

### Where the remaining ticks are

- **~6,350 startup** on 16^3 (MUL blocked on the gate while LOADER drains). Irreducible without
  letting MUL touch the ring during load — that is the bug that cost 19/20 the first time.
- **The ring lap is a floor of ~300 ticks for small cases.** `rf`(9) + TAIL(6) + `ring`(291) = ~306
  cells, but a 2x2x2 case only has `M(K+1)+1 = 7` tokens in it, so MUL is starved and each of the N
  laps costs 300 ticks regardless. 2x2x2 = 851 ticks is ~301 gate + 2 x 300 lap. **The ring drum
  only needs 273 cells** (`M(K+1)+1` at 16x16) — trimming 300 -> 275 is worth ~25 ticks per lap on
  every small case. Same for `gate` at 135 cells against a documented requirement of ~84.
- **ACC now has two empty interior rows** (y38, y39). ACC could be 22x11 instead of 22x13, which
  lifts the whole bottom band two rows -> 48x46. Left for the human packer; footprint alone would
  take 30.2M to ~27.7M.

### Dead ends recorded

- **`U` for a free two-way dispatch** (products on one pipe, markers on another; `U` turns away from
  the pipe it read) would cut the hot loop 10 -> 8 cells, ~15%. **It is not safe**: `U`/`R` break
  ties by reading order, not arrival order, so a marker sitting ready while a product is also ready
  reorders the stream.
- **Making PAD an even `0` so it flows through ACCUM as a no-op product** removes the PAD branch
  entirely, but INIT then cannot tell the end of `t=0` from a genuinely zero product (entries may be
  0). Needs the accumulator ring pre-primed with K zeros, and nobody downstream of LOADER knows K.
- **`X` on sign to split PAD from ENDPAD in one cell** instead of `]`+`x` saves one cell per marker,
  but needs PAD = -1, and PADK has no spare interior column for the `N`.

### Next priced hypothesis: restore A-drum capacity inside 43x43

### Revised tick hypothesis: restore the v10 ring split

v12 shifted MUL left but left TAIL at x34, changing the cycle from v10's `rf=10, ring=272` to

### Next footprint hypothesis: narrow PADK by moving its loop spine

A 42-column layout is otherwise blocked by PADK's east wall. Its loop spine currently returns at
interior x2. Move that spine to x1: setup's westbound row travels one extra cell, while the repeated
loop shifts left one cell unchanged. This moves the exit branch `d` from x6 to x5, so the exact

### Revised PADK hypothesis: vertical countdown loop
